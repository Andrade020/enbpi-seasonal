% ABSTRACT
% ============================================================
We study the coverage properties of the Ensemble batch
Prediction Intervals (EnbPI) algorithm of \citet{xu2023conformal}
under seasonal heteroskedasticity --- the empirically common
setting in which the variance of forecast errors varies
systematically by calendar season.  We show that pooled
calibration introduces a persistent, season-dependent bias
of magnitude $2b_{s^*}$ in the per-season coverage gap,
where $b_{s^*}$ measures the discrepancy between the
season-specific residual CDF and the pooled mixture CDF.
This bias does not vanish as the training sample grows,
causing systematic undercoverage in high-volatility seasons
and overcoverage in low-volatility seasons.

To correct for this bias, we propose \texttt{EnbPI-S}, a
stratified extension of EnbPI that maintains $S$ separate
calibration buffers --- one per calendar season --- while
keeping the bootstrap training phase and sliding-window
update of the original algorithm unchanged.  We prove that
the per-season coverage gap of \texttt{EnbPI-S} converges
to zero at rate $O(\sqrt{\log T_s / T_s})$, where
$T_s = T/S$ is the per-stratum sample size, and derive
a finite-sample dominance condition that identifies when
stratification yields tighter intervals than pooling.

Simulation experiments compare three methods ---
SARIMA-Gaussian, Pooled EnbPI, and \texttt{EnbPI-S} ---
revealing a progressive improvement.
SARIMA-Gaussian suffers from two compounding failures
(Gaussian tails and pooled variance), achieving only $66\%$
coverage in September at the $90\%$ nominal level.
Pooled EnbPI corrects the distributional assumption via
empirical quantiles, raising high-volatility coverage to $79\%$.
\texttt{EnbPI-S} further corrects the seasonal calibration
bias: high-volatility coverage rises to $84\%$, and the
cross-season coverage spread collapses from $0.24$ (SARIMA)
to $0.02$ --- a twelve-fold reduction.
An application to monthly Brazilian consumer inflation
(IPCA, 1995--2024) illustrates that \texttt{EnbPI-S}
correctly assigns wider intervals to high-uncertainty
months and narrower intervals to predictable months,
while the pooled method applies a uniform bandwidth.
A second application to Brazilian monthly export growth
($T_s = 35$) confirms the method in the adequate-sample
regime: both methods achieve coverage within $3.3$
percentage points of nominal, and \texttt{EnbPI-S}
produces a factor-of-$2.3$ width variation across seasons,
with the widest interval in February (soybean harvest-onset)
and the narrowest in July.

\bigskip

\noindent\textbf{Resumo:}
Estudamos as propriedades de cobertura do algoritmo
EnbPI \citep{xu2023conformal} sob heteroscedasticidade
sazonal --- o cenário em que a variância dos erros de previsão
varia sistematicamente por estação do calendário.
Mostramos que a calibração conjunta (pooled) introduz um
viés persistente de magnitude $2b_{s^*}$ na cobertura
por estação.  Para corrigir esse viés, propomos o
\texttt{EnbPI-S}, uma extensão estratificada do EnbPI
que mantém $S$ buffers de calibração separados --- um
por mês do calendário --- preservando integralmente a
fase de treinamento por bootstrap e a atualização em
janela deslizante do algoritmo original.
Provamos que a lacuna de cobertura por estação de
\texttt{EnbPI-S} converge a zero na taxa
$O(\sqrt{\log T_s / T_s})$ e derivamos uma condição
de dominância de amostra finita.
Experimentos de Monte Carlo com três métodos
(SARIMA-Gaussiano, EnbPI agrupado e \texttt{EnbPI-S})
revelam melhora progressiva: o SARIMA cobre apenas $66\%$
no pior mês; o EnbPI agrupado eleva isso para $79\%$;
o \texttt{EnbPI-S} chega a $84\%$ e reduz a dispersão
de cobertura entre estações em doze vezes.
Uma aplicação ao componente de Alimentação no domicílio do
IPCA (BCB série 1635, 1995--2024) ilustra que
\texttt{EnbPI-S} produz intervalos mais largos nos meses
de ajuste de safra (maio--junho) e mais estreitos nos
meses estáveis (fevereiro, outubro), enquanto o método
conjunto aplica uma largura uniforme ao longo do ano.
