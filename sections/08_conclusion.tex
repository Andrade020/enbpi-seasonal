\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

%------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Summary}
\label{sec:conclusion:summary}
%------------------------------------------------------------

We identified a structural limitation of the pooled EnbPI
algorithm \citep{xu2023conformal} when applied to seasonally
heteroskedastic time series: pooled calibration introduces a
persistent, season-dependent coverage bias of magnitude
$2b_{s^*}$ that does not vanish as the training sample grows
(Proposition~\ref{prop:pooled_gap}).  We proposed
\texttt{EnbPI-S}, a minimal modification that routes each
LOO residual to a season-specific calibration buffer, eliminating
this bias while preserving the bootstrap training procedure,
the sliding-window update, and the asymmetric line-search
optimisation of the original algorithm.

Theoretically, we proved that the per-season coverage gap of
\texttt{EnbPI-S} decays at rate $O(\sqrt{\log T_s / T_s})$,
where $T_s = T/\Sper$ is the per-stratum training size, and
derived a finite-sample dominance condition linking the
benefit of stratification to the size of $b_{s^*}$ and the
training sample length (Proposition~\ref{prop:strat_dominance}).
A companion bound characterises the interval width reduction
achievable in low-volatility seasons (Theorem~\ref{thm:width_bound}).

Empirically, the simulation study confirmed that
\texttt{EnbPI-S} produces more uniform coverage across seasons
than pooled EnbPI: for $T = 480$ months and $\sigma_{\mathrm{high}}/\sigma_{\mathrm{low}} = 2$,
the per-season coverage range shrinks from $\approx 0.18$
(pooled) to $\approx 0.02$ (stratified), and the worst-case
per-season coverage improves from $\approx 0.78$ to
$\approx 0.83$.  The interval widths for \texttt{EnbPI-S}
correctly reflect the seasonal $\sigma$ ratio.
Two empirical applications used the same test window
(January 2015 -- December 2024).
The food-at-home IPCA application ($T_s = 20$) demonstrated
clear width adaptation (ratio $1.78\times$).
The Brazilian export growth application ($T_s = 35$,
BCB series 1402) confirmed the method in the
adequate-sample regime: both methods achieved coverage
within $3.3$ percentage points of nominal, and
\texttt{EnbPI-S} produced a factor-of-$2.3$ width variation ---
correctly assigning a $13.8\%$ interval to February
(soybean harvest-onset) and a $6.0\%$ interval to July ---
while the pooled baseline applied a constant $\approx 10.2\%$
throughout.

%------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion:limitations}
%------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Minimum stratum size.}
The main practical limitation of \texttt{EnbPI-S} is its
requirement for a sufficient per-stratum sample size.
Our simulation study (Experiment E2) suggests that
$T_s \gtrsim 30$--$40$ observations per season are needed
for the stratified method to outperform pooled calibration
in terms of overall coverage.  For monthly data with
$\Sper = 12$ seasons, this requires $T \gtrsim 360$--$480$
months (30--40 years), which may not be available in
all applications.

\paragraph{Fixed seasonal period.}
The current framework assumes a known, fixed seasonal period
$\Sper$.  In practice, the relevant cycle may be quarterly
($\Sper = 4$), weekly ($\Sper = 52$), or irregular (e.g.\
fiscal years that do not align with calendar years).  The
algorithm extends immediately to any integer $\Sper$, but
selection of $\Sper$ is left to the practitioner.

\paragraph{Structural breaks.}
The sliding-window update adapts to slow drift in the
seasonal volatility structure, but it is not designed to
handle abrupt structural breaks.  The undercoverage observed
in the IPCA empirical application during 2020--22 reflects
such a break: the COVID-era inflation dynamics lie outside
the support of the training distribution.  Robust
recalibration after detected breaks is an open problem.

%------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Future Directions}
\label{sec:conclusion:future}
%------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Combination with SPCI.}
\citet{xu2023sequential} proposed SPCI, which exploits residual
autocorrelation by training a quantile random forest on the
history of residuals.  \texttt{EnbPI-S} and SPCI address
orthogonal sources of miscoverage: SPCI corrects for serial
dependence, \texttt{EnbPI-S} corrects for seasonal scale
variation.  A combined method could apply the quantile-forest
step within each seasonal buffer, potentially achieving both
autocorrelation-adjusted and season-calibrated intervals.
We leave the formalisation of such a combined estimator to
future work.

\paragraph{Adaptive stratum selection.}
Rather than stratifying by the calendar month, one could
stratify by a data-driven cluster of months with similar
residual distributions.  A Gaussian mixture model or a
$k$-means clustering of the monthly empirical quantile
vectors would allow the number of strata to be chosen
from data, trading off stratification bias against
per-stratum sample size.

\paragraph{Coverage-corrected line search.}
As documented in Experiment E2, the minimum-width line search
can be unreliable for small $T_s$ due to the heavy tails of
the empirical distribution.  A finite-sample correction ---
for instance, replacing the empirical quantile $Q_p$ by the
conformally adjusted version $Q_{\lceil (T_s+1)p \rceil / T_s}$
--- would provide exact coverage guarantees at the cost of
slightly wider intervals and merits further study.

\paragraph{Beyond monthly seasonality.}
The framework applies directly to quarterly, daily, or intraday
seasonality by adjusting $\Sper$.  For high-frequency data
with large $\Sper$ (e.g.\ hourly electricity demand with
$\Sper = 8736$), the per-stratum sample sizes would be
very small and alternative grouping strategies would be
necessary.  The theoretical analysis extends to this regime
with minor adjustments to the DKW-based argument in
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.
